Getting to a real answer — not a guess.

Milestone 1 had one job: figure out who almost™ is really for and what makes it different, based on evidence — not on what we assume or hope.

This is not a failure. This is exactly where a brand-new app of this kind sits before it finds its footing. But the numbers tell us something important, and we have to say it plainly:

The real problem, named

The social posts, the waitlist ads, and the launch so far have brought people to almost™ — but they haven't solved the one thing that makes an app like this work: getting enough of the right people in the same place at the same time. Without that, there's no one to match with, and people leave. That's why only a handful stayed after a week.

Here's the encouraging part. Every app like this hit this exact wall. Tinder launched on one college campus. Hinge started in one city. Bumble did the same. None of them chased big install numbers first — they packed the right people into one place until real connections started happening, then spread from there. almost™ is standing where the giants once stood. The move now is the move they made.

Why this matters for your goal

Your goal is 1,000 users, matches, and repeat users. Here's the trap: 1,000 users spread across 200 airports is worse than 200 who all move through the same one. The scattered 1,000 never match — the app feels empty and people leave. The focused 200 match, come back, and give you real proof. So the smart order is: build a crowd in one place first, get matches and repeats happening, then grow the number. Same goal — the right way to reach it.

The key finding

None of them can move into almost™'s spot without breaking their own app. Happn can't come to the airport without abandoning tracking. JetLatch chose "airport dating" and the swipe grammar almost™ rejects. Seatlr is a group chat by design. And the three real app competitors are all still pre-traction — nobody has won this yet. The turn we didn't take

The obvious move — given almost™'s romantic origin — was to lead as a dating app for travelers. We looked hard at it and turned away. JetLatch already owns that lane and built it on swiping, which almost™ isn't. Leading with romance would walk almost™ straight into a fight on someone else's ground, as the smaller, later app. The harder, open door is the one worth taking.

What this points to
almost™ should not lead as a dating app. The open, winnable spot is the one nobody's taken: the intentional connection app for travelers — for any kind of real connection while you travel.

Testing feedback also flagged useful fixes to note for later: managing several trips in one place, alerts when someone overlaps with you, and profile verification for trust.

05The decision — who almost™ is for

The open traveler.

Here's the big shift. almost™'s user isn't defined by what they're looking for — professional, friendship, or romance. It's defined by how they travel: open to meeting someone good along the way.

The primary audience
The open traveler — anyone on a trip who'd welcome a real connection instead of passing through alone. Professional, friendship, and romance aren't three different users. They're three things that can happen when an open traveler meets the right person.
The turn we didn't take

We almost narrowed this to solo travelers who feel lonely — one early reply pointed there. But it broke fast: it leaves out couples and families who'd love to meet others, and plenty of solo travelers actually want quiet. "Lonely solo traveler" was too small. "Open traveler" holds up — it fits anyone, on the trips when they're open, and lets the app sit quiet on the trips when they're not.

This one idea quietly fixes a lot. It ends the "dating or networking?" tug-of-war — you don't have to choose. It's the same way you already describe almost™: "a way to meet people when you travel." This just gives that a sharper edge. And it helps the empty-app problem, because "I'd welcome meeting someone good" is a much easier yes than "I want to date" — more people saying yes means more matches, sooner.

Why professional networking comes up first — and why that's fine

Early users keep asking for a professional focus. Here's the real reason: light professional networking feels safe. "I saw you're headed to the same conference" costs nothing to send. Note it's the light version that's easy — deliberately hunting for a business partner or investor is just as heavy and guarded as looking for a romantic partner. So easy professional connection is the front door. Once people are connecting, friendships and romance follow naturally. Lead with the easy door. Keep the rest open.

One honest limit: the app today is built for one person at a time. It can't yet handle "we're a couple who'd like to meet another couple," or families meeting families. Those are real audiences worth having — but they need a small change to the app first. So couples and families are Phase 2, and we build the launch around what the MVP can do today.

The position, in one breath
almost™ is the way to meet the people you're already going to cross paths with when you travel — before you arrive, and before the moment passes.

Every other app sells you the connection. almost™ names the cost of missing it:

You almost met your next business partner. You almost met a real friend. You almost met the person who'd change your life. Almost — because you were in the same place at the same time, and no one had a way to know.

The feeling almost™ owns

This flips the whole mood. Happn leaves you with regret — "you missed your chance, it's over." almost™ puts the chance right in front of you, while there's still time to take it. It fits your own line, too — airports are becoming the new downtown. A place where anything could happen, if someone makes the move.

Your romantic story is safe — it just isn't the headline

almost™ was born from a romantic near-miss, and that stays the soul of the brand. We're not throwing it away. We're protecting it as the rarest, most magical thing almost™ can do — and we lead with the easy, everyday reason people open the app, because that's what builds the crowd that makes the magic even possible. Lead with the door. Keep the soul.

The full positioning statement — for the record
For people who travel and believe the best connections come from being in the same place at the same time — but who are done with swiping, done with being tracked, and done leaving those moments to chance —

almost™ is the intentional connection app that shows you who you're about to cross paths with, so you can choose to meet — for business, for friendship, or for something more — before you land.

Unlike Happn (accidental, past-tense, romance-only, built on location tracking), JetLatch (an airport dating app built on swiping), and Seatlr (a group chat room for a whole city),

almost™ owns the deliberate, one-to-one moment of overlap — planned, not stumbled into; never tracked; open to every kind of connection worth making while you travel.

07The product decision ahead

One small change. A massive difference.

There's one adjustment to the app that would unlock far more of what it's for. It's small to make and big in effect — and it's yours to decide with your developer. My job is to make it clear.

Right now, almost™ only connects two people if they're at the same airport at the same time. That's a narrow window. Most trips won't line up that perfectly — which means most people won't find a match — which is a big reason the app feels empty.

The fix is smaller than it sounds. The app already asks for a place and a date range. Today that place is the airport. The change is simply to let it be the destination — the city you're actually in — not just the airport you passed through. Same format, same screen. Just a wider "where."

The exact size and timing is your call with your developer — and this whole plan is built to work either way, so nothing is blocked while you decide. One note for whenever a "here right now" feature comes: it should be something people choose to share, with a time stamp — never silent tracking. That protects almost™'s no-tracking promise.

08The move that matters most

Build the crowd. Density is the move.

Everything so far points to one conclusion. almost™ doesn't need more scattered downloads. It needs a real crowd of the right people in one place — and building that is the single most important thing right now.

The conclusion
The make-or-break move for almost™ today is density-marketing — concentrating the right people in one place until real matches happen — then copying that recipe to the next place. Not more awareness. Not more scattered installs. A crowd, in one spot, first.

This isn't a hunch. It's the exact path the biggest apps in this space took — Tinder on one campus, Hinge and Bumble in one city each. They won by going narrow and deep before wide. almost™ gets to use the same proven playbook.

And here's the important insight about how density actually gets built, because it isn't obvious: you can't manufacture a crowd at the airport directly. You build it by concentrating a community in a place — through real, in-person connection — and that community then carries almost™ into the airports and cities they travel through. The community creates the overlap. Start with people in one place; the travel density follows.

The natural place to start is Nashville — home base, where you're building your network — and your Founding Travelers video call on August 24 is the perfect first moment to begin gathering that community.

What comes next

Exactly how we concentrate the right crowd in Nashville — the community engine, the first event, the partnerships, and how we turn the first room into momentum — is the plan we build next. This milestone proves what the move is. The next one builds how.
