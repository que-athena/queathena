Where Things Stand

Your business works. The market got interrupted.

CueShip solves a real, everyday problem: Jamaicans want to shop from US stores like Shein, Amazon, and countless others, but shipping directly to the island is expensive, slow, or simply not offered. CueShip gives them a US address, receives their orders, and delivers them home affordably. That's a service people need constantly, and for three years it grew on the strongest marketing there is: word of mouth.

Then Hurricane Melissa changed things. It disrupted the island, daily life, and people's spending, and the steady flow of online orders slowed with it. Shipments that once ran daily now run a few times a week. The customers didn't leave because of anything you did. Their world was turned upside down, and shopping took a back seat to recovery.

Here's what that means, and why it's good news. The need didn't disappear. Jamaicans still want US products at prices that make sense, and as the island recovers, that demand comes back. The job now isn't to build a market from nothing. It's to reconnect with the shoppers who already rely on you, reach the many who haven't found you yet, and build a marketing engine so CueShip never has to depend on word of mouth alone again.
The Opportunity

Win on price and trust, then tap what's next.

CueShip's advantage is real: you make US shopping affordable for Jamaicans who would otherwise pay astronomical direct-shipping costs or go without. That's a clear, everyday value the big carriers don't offer this community. The marketing job is to make sure every Jamaican who shops online knows CueShip exists and trusts it to deliver.

Most of your growth has come from people telling their friends. That's powerful, but it left the business exposed when the market slowed, because there was no marketing engine to fall back on. This engagement builds that engine, so CueShip is never again relying on word of mouth alone.

And there's an untapped market worth exploring. US-based Jamaicans, the diaspora, could use CueShip to send goods home, especially as the island recovers. That's a second audience most forwarders overlook. Part of the strategy is deciding whether and how to pursue it, so you strengthen the core business and can open a new one at the same time.
